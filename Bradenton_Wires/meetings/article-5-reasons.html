5 Reasons Meeting Planners Love the Bradenton Area.

Hosting an event in the Bradenton Area that's equal parts fun and successful is a breeze, thanks to our state-of-the-art venue, a connected headquarters hotel, and an expert support team. Here's what planners cite when they tell us why they came back.

By Jennifer M. · VP of Sales, BACVB · Updated April 2026
CONVENTION CENTER · MANATEE RIVER

I've sat across the table from a lot of meeting planners. The conversation usually starts the same way: "I've been told to look at the Bradenton Area, but I don't really know it." By the end of the conversation — and especially by the end of the site visit — the planner is sketching room blocks on a notepad. Here's why that pattern is so consistent.

1. The location actually works.

Sarasota–Bradenton International (SRQ) is twenty-five minutes from the Convention Center and offers nonstop service from more than thirty cities. For groups that don't pencil with SRQ alone, Tampa International (TPA) and St. Pete-Clearwater (PIE) are inside an hour, giving you genuine overflow without the routing gymnastics that destinations farther south require.

Once your attendees are on the ground, the math gets simpler: the venue, the headquarters hotel, and 1,500 nearby rooms are all within a ten-minute radius. No long shuttle rides between hotel block and general session.

2. The venue is flexible — and on the river.

The Bradenton Area Convention Center is 60,000 sq ft of flexible meeting space on the Manatee River, with eight distinct rooms ranging from a 22,000 sq ft divisible ballroom to 14-person boardrooms. Floor plans here.

The river isn't a backdrop. It's a tool. The Riverview Hall has floor-to-ceiling water views — the difference between "we held a banquet" and "we held an event." Sunset Terrace, our 4,000 sq ft outdoor space, is the most-requested welcome reception location in the destination.

3. In-house catering that reads like the destination.

Gulf Islands Hospitality is the exclusive in-house caterer for the BACC. They cook Floribbean — Floridian seafood meets Caribbean kitchen — at convention scale. Your menu reads like the place your attendees flew to, because it is.

This is where I lose planners who came in skeptical: a tasting where conch fritters arrive next to dry-aged steak next to a vegan grain bowl, and all three are good.

"The space worked, the staff worked harder, and our attendees came back with sand still in their shoes. We're rebooking 2027."
— Director of Events, National Trade Association

4. A connected headquarters hotel.

The Palmetto Marriott Resort & Spa sits on the same campus as the BACC. 266 guest rooms, an additional 20,000 sq ft of meeting space, golf, a full spa, and three restaurants. For groups under 500, the Marriott functions as a self-contained venue. For larger programs, it pairs cleanly with the BACC.

The campus walk between the hotel lobby and the BACC pre-function foyer is under three minutes — covered. Rain plan, sorted.

5. A real destination after the agenda.

This is the one planners under-weight at the front, and over-weight at the end of every program: where attendees are when they're not in your sessions matters. Twenty-five minutes from the BACC, you'll find Anna Maria Island, Coquina Beach, the historic fishing village of Cortez, and a downtown Riverwalk lined with restaurants.

This isn't a casino-corridor destination where attendees leave the venue and immediately spend money in ways that don't resemble fun. It's a coast that built itself for people who actually wanted to be there. Spouses thank you. Senior leaders rebook. And — bonus — the planning team gets to extend a day at the end of the program too.

Bonus: an off-site that nobody else has.

The Powel Crosley Estate is a 1929 Mediterranean Revival mansion on Sarasota Bay, ten miles from the BACC. We pair it with the convention center for the welcome reception, the donor dinner, the executive board program — the moments where you need the room to feel different.

It's the kind of off-site that makes planners look like heroes.

Where to start
