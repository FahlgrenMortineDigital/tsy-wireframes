8 Ways to Spend an Extra Day in the Bradenton Area.

Headed to the Bradenton Area on business? Consider flying in early or extending your stay by a day. Here are eight ways to use that extra time without having to think too hard about it.

By Marcus T. · Senior Event Coordinator, BACVB · Updated April 2026
ATTENDEE · BRIDGE STREET

Most attendees fly in the morning of and out the afternoon they finish. They leave the destination right when the meeting room ends. The most common feedback we get from attendees who built in an extra day: "I wish I'd done two." Here are eight uses for the extra time, sorted by what kind of trip you want.

  1. An island morning. Twenty-five minutes from the Convention Center is Anna Maria Island — seven miles of barrier island, free trolley, sugar-white sand. Coquina Beach for parking, Bean Point for sunrise, lunch on Pine Avenue. More on Anna Maria.
  2. A working-village seafood lunch. Cortez is one of Florida's last commercial fishing villages. Eat at Star Fish Company — order at the window, eat on the dock, watch the boats unload. Ten minutes from the BACC. More on Cortez.
  3. A kayak through Robinson Preserve. 487 acres of restored coastal habitat. Mangrove tunnels, bird rookeries, manatee viewing. Guided kayak tours run multiple times daily, around $45/person. Built for half-day attendees with no rental car required.
  4. A round of golf. The Palmetto Marriott has 18 holes onsite. Lakewood Ranch (15 minutes inland) has 54 more across three public courses. Group rates available for blocks of 8+.
  5. A spa morning. Headquarters Marriott full-service spa, on-property. Block 90-minute slots for groups via your CSM. The St. Regis Longboat Key spa is 30 minutes south for the higher-end version.
  6. A river-and-art afternoon. Walk the Bradenton Riverwalk · Bishop Museum of Science & Nature · gallery hop on Old Main Street · brewery on Motorworks · dinner at Pier 22. Three hours, no rental needed if your hotel is downtown.
  7. The car-light loop. The Gulf Islands Ferry runs Bradenton Riverwalk to Bridge Street year-round. Forty-five minutes on the water, sunset return, dinner reservations on either end.
  8. A photographer's golden hour. Cortez Beach for sunset over the Gulf. The Anna Maria City Pier looking back at the island. Robinson Preserve observation tower for the 360°. All inside a 25-minute drive.

If you've only got 4 hours

Anna Maria Island is the highest-density "real Florida" experience for the time. Park at Coquina, take the trolley north, walk Pine Avenue, sunset on Bean Point, dinner at the Sandbar. Done.

If you've got a full day

Pair Cortez with Robinson Preserve in the morning, ferry to the Riverwalk in the afternoon, dinner downtown. The whole loop is car-light if you stay near the BACC.

Either way: build the extra day in. We promise you'll thank yourself.
