Floribbean 101 — the only food guide you need.

Conch fritters, hogfish, key lime, and the Cortez seafood you can't get anywhere else. A starter map to what to order, where to order it, and what makes Floribbean a real cuisine instead of a marketing word.

By Chef Marisol · Bridge Street · Updated March 2026
FLORIBBEAN PLATING

Most people hear "Floribbean" and assume it's a tourism word someone made up to sell dinner. It isn't. It's how the Florida West Coast has been cooking since the families in Cortez started bringing fish to shore in the 1880s — Floridian seafood with a Caribbean kitchen, plated within sight of the boat the catch came off.

Here's what you actually need to know before your first dinner in the Bradenton Area.

What Floribbean actually is

Strip away the menu copy, and Floribbean is three things in one bowl:

  • Florida seafood: hogfish, mahi, grouper, snapper, stone crab, conch — caught locally, not shipped frozen.
  • Caribbean kitchen: jerk rub, curry, mango aioli, mojo, plantains, scotch-bonnet heat.
  • Old-Florida sides: hush puppies, key lime, hearts of palm, citrus, hot honey.

Pair those three and you get something like blackened mahi over yuca with mango-habanero salsa. Or hogfish ceviche on plantain chips. Or stone crab claws cracked tableside with key lime mustard.

What to order, in order

Start with conch fritters.

If you're new to this coast and you only try one thing, try conch fritters. Crisp outside, custard-soft inside, with whatever spiced aioli the kitchen makes that day. The best ones I've had on the islands were at Eat Here Restaurant in Holmes Beach — they disappear off the table.

Get the catch of the day.

"Catch of the day" is a real phrase here. Ask the server what came in this morning, then order whatever they answer with. Hogfish is the local prize — sweet, mild, very Florida. The Reef House on Bridge Street builds half their menu around it.

Save room for stone crab (October–May).

Stone crab is a Florida thing — the claws are harvested seasonally and the rest of the crab is returned to the water to regrow. Cortez is the heart of stone crab country. The Cortez Stone Crab & Music Festival in February is the village at full tilt.

End with key lime pie.

Real Florida key lime pie is a yellowish custard, not green, and it's tart enough to make your teeth feel awake. The Reef House makes a version with hot-honey graham crust that I haven't found anywhere else.

Where to eat it — the short list

I could give you twenty restaurants. I'll give you four that show the range:

  • Star Fish Company · Cortez — order at the window, eat at a picnic table on the dock. The seafood was swimming this morning.
  • Tide Tables · Cortez — hyper-local Florida coastal kitchen, hidden behind a fish house. Reservations.
  • The Reef House · Bridge Street — Floribbean date-night, deck over the pass.
  • Sandbar Restaurant · Anna Maria — toes-in-the-sand, sunset over your plate.

For the deeper list, see the Floribbean restaurant directory.

One last thing

If you want to see where it comes from, drive ten minutes to Cortez Village in the morning. Walk the docks, watch the boats unload, then order what they brought in. Floribbean started here. We didn't know it had a name.
